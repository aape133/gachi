Miles apart,

gachi is an app for jamming with others over the internet. Your sound goes straight to each other, never through a server. The app, the VST3 plugin and the connection server are all open source.

Just what a jam needs, done right

Direct, no server in between

Sound goes straight between players with no relay server. It's sent uncompressed at 16 or 24 bits, and the buffer adjusts itself to the connection. For each person you can see where the delay comes from.

Up to 16 people

Join with a six-character code, an invite link or a QR code. If the host leaves, the next person takes over, and if the internet drops for a moment, you rejoin on your own.

Per-track recording

Each player is saved to their own WAV or FLAC file, along with the mix exactly as you heard it. Choose who gets recorded.

One metronome for everyone

The whole room hears the click at the same moment. Set the BPM, time signature and count-in, and everyone hears it the same.

Works with your DAW

Put the VST3 plugin at the end of a DAW track to jam with your effects on and hear the others mixed in right there. It adds no latency to your DAW.

End-to-end encrypted

Sound and chat are encrypted so only the people in the room can open them. Room passwords are never sent to the server.

Open source

All the code is out in the open

The app, the VST3 plugin and the connection server are all on GitHub. Anyone can check how it works, change it, and run their own connection server. Licensed under GPLv3.

Start in three steps

  1. 1

    Install

    Run the installer, then pick your audio interface in Settings.

  2. 2

    Create a room

    Choose a name, create a room, and send the code or invite link to your friends.

  3. 3

    Play together

    When your friends join with the code, you hear each other right away.

What you need

  • Windows 10 / 11 (64-bit)
  • An audio interface with an ASIO driver (with a DAW, the DAW handles the device)
  • A wired connection is recommended. Bigger rooms need plenty of upload speed.

FAQ

How much delay is there?

It depends on distance, your internet and your audio devices. Nearby, like within the same city, on wired internet, it's short enough to hear each other and play in time. The app splits each person's delay into their device, the network, the buffer and your own device, so you can see what to improve.

Is it free?

Yes. There are no ads, and all the source code is public.

I can't connect

gachi connects you directly, without a relay server. If both of you are on mobile hotspots, or on a network that blocks direct connections, like at work or school, it may not connect. The app then tells you why and what to try. If your router has no UPnP, forward the fixed port from the app's settings on your router.

How is my privacy handled?

Sound and chat travel encrypted and never pass through the server. The connection server only introduces you at the start and doesn't keep a record of who connected.

The app never shows other people's IP addresses, and its troubleshooting log keeps only part of an address. Still, to send sound straight to each other, the computers need each other's address, so someone using network analysis tools could find the IP addresses of people in the same room. Keep this in mind when playing with people you don't know.
